# EXP-0003 — Preregistered chronological evaluation and uncertainty protocol

**Status:** `PROPOSED; SOURCE-FREE IMPLEMENTATION; NO REAL-DATA RUN`.  
**Config:** [configs/EXP-0003.json](configs/EXP-0003.json).  
**Scope:** dedicated reusable harness in `src/latent_risk/evaluation/`, independent of EXP-0002. This is a **pre-registration of future tests**, not evidence that an empirical benchmark has been executed.

## Scientific question and estimands

For one *fixed, named, country-specific* observed financial-stress target, how do prospective-origin level forecasts compare against persistence, a fitted AR(1), one-sided EWMA and a volatility-responsive level heuristic? For any *separately reconstructed latent index*, the estimation method, sign, scale and dataset vintage form part of the target identifier; cross-index RMSE cannot compare different physical targets.

Primary loss is \(L_{m,t,h} = (y_{t+h} - \hat y^{(m)}_{t+h\mid t})^2\). Within a common country/target/horizon, the primary estimand is average out-of-origin MSE and the paired difference in MSE versus persistence; RMSE and MAE are descriptive complements. No cross-country raw-loss averaging. No event-detection, economic profitability or predictive superiority claims are preregistered.

## Information set and release-date policy

Time labels are **observation dates**, measured at **end-of-day** for this numerical protocol. At an origin \(t\), `run_backtest` passes to `fit` only target observations with `observation_date <= t`, and, if declared in `provided_release_dates` mode, `available_date <= t`. Inner folds apply the same cutoff at their own origins; an inner forecast target must also have been released by the **outer** origin to enter hyperparameter selection. Rows lacking genuinely audited availability dates use `current_vintage`, meaning retrospective numerical chronology only, **not** an actual historical point-in-time information set. Provided dates are user assertions; they are not an authenticated vintage archive, and even a verified release date alone does not recover an older revised value.

A horizon \(h\in\{1,5,21\}\) means \(h\) **observed CISS rows after the origin**, not (h) calendar days, exchange trading sessions or months. Different-country calendars are not assumed synchronized. To use a monthly study, create and preregister a *different* calendar and outcome protocol, rather than relabel CISS h=21 as one month.

## Outer/inner folds and fair comparison

Proposed *exploratory CISS* scope: US, UK and euro-area ECB CISS histories separately; Mexico and Japan **have no matching series in this CISS experiment**, and are not replaced with fabricated surrogates. Earliest origin 2008-01-01, minimum 504 *available* observations, expanding training, stride 21 source observations. Repeat with a prespecified rolling window if justified, but report separately.

Each outer training prefix is partitioned into earlier inner chronological origins; minimum 252 training observations, stride 21, and inner targets at horizon \(h\) restricted to data known by the outer origin. Each candidate model is reinstantiated and fitted from scratch for every inner evaluation and the outer forecast. Tune EWMA `alpha` from `0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.85, 1`; compare two window lengths (21 and 63) for volatility-adjusted persistence; deterministic lexicographic tie breaks. All candidate preprocessing, factor loadings, signed scaling, country weights, smoothing parameters and thresholds **must** be fitted inside that candidate's `fit`; precomputed full-sample feature/factor transforms are inadmissible. The current narrow API takes one-dimensional target histories only: multi-country/exogenous integration is **proposed**, not implemented.

**Data-matching invariant:** every model must forecast exactly the same `(origin_date, target_date)` rows with identical actual values for each country/target/horizon/scale/vintage. Missing predictions cause failure rather than selective comparison. Each result row records the immutable `dataset_vintage` identifier, origin, as-of label, fit cutoff, training count, selected candidate, inner-fold count, target identity, vintage policy and horizon units.

## Baselines and uncertainty

- **Persistence:** `y_t` at last released observation; strong random-walk-level benchmark.
- **AR(1):** OLS on the last at most 504 training-only points, recursively iterated to the specified observed-point horizon.
- **One-sided EWMA:** gain selected on inner h-step loss; flat multi-step level forecast.
- **Volatility-adjusted persistence:** `y_t + 0.5 * std(diff(y[-window-1:])) * sqrt(h)`. This is a **positive-tail stress heuristic**, not a conventional volatility model for conditional mean. Never describe it as a fitted GARCH model or as volatility itself being the target.

Compute MSE, RMSE, MAE separately. Circular moving-block bootstrap intervals resample contiguous origin-indexed loss blocks jointly across matched competitors; proposed 999 replications, seed 20261018, exploratory default block `ceil(n_origins^(1/3))`. Block size denotes *origins* not days or CISS observations. Specify sensitivity to alternate block sizes. Paired squared-loss DM-style comparisons use Newey–West/Bartlett HAC, normal approximation, a declared lag (exploratory default `min(h-1, floor(n/4))`), and two-sided 95% confidence intervals. The default `h-1` rationale assumes consecutive origins; with stride 21 it can be overly conservative. Re-estimation and nonstationarity may require larger or different lags: report sensitivity, do not claim finite-sample exact inference or automatic causal interpretation. Multiple comparisons and overlapping/serially dependent errors remain serious concerns.

## Limits and interpretation

A computationally causal current-vintage backtest is still retrospective, because historical revisions and release times may not be reconstructed. The simple forecaster interface does not itself certify that arbitrary factories avoid outside global state; implementations and exogenous adapters require review and stronger provenance audits. Factor targets may change sign and scale between refits; meaningful cross-index validity requires a common independently observed outcome. Short samples, structural breaks, nested model searching, repeated country/horizon tests and nonstationary loss differences can invalidate asymptotic HAC or stationary bootstrap inference. Performance and forecast-disturbance association: **UNKNOWN** until independently verified live runs.
